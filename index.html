Review

After spending 130 hours with Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave, I can confidently say that this new addition to the series brings everything that older and newer Fire Emblem fans would find enjoyable.

Fire Emblem: Fortunes Weave brings back most of the grid and turn based gameplay that the fans love, and many brand new personal abilities and weapons as well. This is a bit of a spoiler, but for the first time in a Fire Emblem game, there are GUNS. They describe it as "devices that can fire magical bolts of energy, but it's literally just a gun. The art is amazing, as you will see below in the gallery, and the diversity in the characters is nice as well. I think the biggest thing about this game is its story, which has you complete four routes that end up converging in one timeline. Without giving too many spoilers, the basic plot of this game is that you play as Eshmel, or whatever name you decide to give to the main character, and try to go back in time to prevent the ruin of the four lords of Dagda, so that they may later join you in the final battle against the main antagonist. Based on my 130 hours, I would give this game a 8/10. There are some things that I think they could've made WAY better, but the game is still extremely enjoyable and satisfying.

Gameplay

As I said before, the gameplay is what normal Fire Emblem fans are used to, but there are some new mechanics, such as blessings. Basically, you can do temple service during your free time, which lets you use the gods' blessings in battle for certain stat boosts that last for the turn or something completely different, such as opening up fog on maps with fog. This cannot be done infintely, and each blessings costs of different amount of divine sand, which is the currency you use to activate blessings on the map. Another new mechanic is the Blaze Arts, which I personally loved. Each of your four main lords will have a Blaze ability, which is a very strong ability that you can use at the cost of your own hit points. Some of these are insanely satisfying to use, as for one of the lords you can literally throw a giant black fireball across the whole map and leave almost 1/4 of the map lit on fire for the turn. Overall, I think the gameplay is really good.
